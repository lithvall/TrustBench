# The finding that matters more than the PR

Reading the section to place the entry surfaced **eight direct competitors, none of which appear in `competitive/COMPETITIVE-MEMORY.md`.** Several occupy exactly the position TrustBench moved into this morning.

## Occupying the discovery-first position

| Project | Self-description (verbatim from the list) |
|---|---|
| **x402 List** (x402-list.com) | "Agent-first directory of x402 API services with live uptime monitoring and machine-readable discovery for AI agents (JSON API, OpenAPI 3.1, **llms.txt**)" |
| **SmartFlow Mapper API** | "**22,251+** catalogued x402 endpoints with uptime, payment-success, and facilitator metadata. Free tier (100 req/day) + **paid bulk export**" |
| **Cinderwright Discovery Hub** | "Cross-protocol discovery hub indexing **2,771+** AI agent payment services across **x402, MPP, and L402/Lightning**" |

x402 List is TrustBench's new positioning almost word for word, llms.txt included. SmartFlow claims ~14× TrustBench's ranked registry (1,545) **and is already monetising** via paid bulk export — the registry-monetisation problem TrustBench has never solved. Cinderwright ships the cross-protocol coverage that is TrustBench's Phase 5 ambition.

## Occupying the signed-receipt position (Pillar 1)

| Project | Self-description |
|---|---|
| **Tersign** (tersign.ai) | "Neutral evidence layer for x402 agent commerce: seller-signed EIP-712 receipts and agent action records, counter-signed into per-seller hash chains on a public ledger, **verifiable by anyone without an account**, with refunds, deterministic dispute triage, and exportable evidence packs" |
| **Mycelium Trails** | "Post-execution accountability receipts... payment_hash + action_ref (SHA-256 commitment) + **dual-chain anchor (Arbitrum One + Base)**. Verifiable by anyone" |

Tersign's "neutral evidence layer... verifiable by anyone without an account" is TrustBench's receipt thesis, and hash chains + dispute triage + evidence packs are *further developed* than the current envelope. Mycelium anchors on two chains where TrustBench anchors on one.

## Occupying spend caps / policy, and measurement

- **Sentinel** — "Enterprise audit & compliance layer... Budget enforcement (per-call, hourly, daily), structured audit trails, real-time dashboard, public payment explorer" + npm SDK.
- **Paybound** — "Open-source governance proxy... per-agent budgets, circuit breakers, SQLite audit trail."
- **Assay** — "Service-quality oracle for x402: **pays real USDC to probe** machine-payable services on a schedule and scores what actually comes back." This is methodologically stronger than TrustBench's HEAD-probe liveness check, which is the limitation TrustBench documents honestly everywhere.

## What to conclude, and what not to

**Do not over-read these.** They are self-descriptions in an awesome-list. Scale claims are unverified. Given this quarter's pattern — Strata, MAKO, Infopunks and x402route all dormant or dead behind live infrastructure — a meaningful fraction are probably inactive too. And TrustBench's own lesson applies with full force: *presence on a distribution surface is never evidence of conversion through it.* Being listed does not mean they have users.

**Do conclude this:** the competitive picture in `COMPETITIVE-MEMORY.md` was assembled from X, Reddit and direct discovery, and it missed the single densest concentration of direct competitors in the ecosystem — a public README that any agent sweep enumerates first. The discovery blind spot and the competitive blind spot were the same blind spot. Not being in the list meant not reading the list.

That is the strongest argument for the PR, independent of any traffic it brings: **being in the place competitors are listed is how you find out who they are.**
